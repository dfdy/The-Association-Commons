# Contribute to the Commons

[Back to the project](README.md) · [Documentation](docs/README.md)

The DDC is a working proposal. People who use, question, translate, and test it help
shape what it becomes. You do not need to be a programmer, academic, or member of an
established organisation to point out a barrier or contribute an idea.

## Choose a useful starting point

- **Discuss a practice.** Explain what an association has tried, whose participation
  it enabled or excluded, and what people learned. Distinguish an experience from
  a proposal that has not yet been tested.
- **Improve an explanation.** Identify a sentence, unfamiliar term, or missing step.
  Say who the explanation should help and suggest wording if you can.
- **Review a language edition.** Work with speakers of that language. Identify the
  passage and explain the intended meaning; machine output is a draft, not evidence
  of community approval.
- **Identify an access barrier.** Describe the task, device or assistive technology,
  what happened, and what would have made participation possible.
- **Review research.** Identify the record and source, explain your reasoning, and
  keep uncertainty visible. The legal records and governance patterns need review
  before anyone relies on them.
- **Improve the software.** Start with a small change that people can understand,
  test, discuss, and undo if necessary.

There is no requirement to agree with every part of the framework to raise a
question. Explain the issue and its effects on people, and make room for others to
disagree or offer a different experience.

## Share a question or proposal

Use the [project's issue tracker](https://github.com/dfdy/The-Democratic-Development-Commons/issues)
for public problems and suggestions. A GitHub account is needed to post there.
Include the page or feature, what you were trying to do, and the change you propose.
Avoid including another person's name or story without their permission.

On a running copy of the website, the **Contact** page and **Join** form accept
participation enquiries and translation proposals. Submissions go to the operator
of that copy, not to a central DDC inbox. They do not create membership or send an
automatic email. The repository currently supplies a local setup, with public
operator/contact arrangements still pending. Do not post private submissions,
passwords, security secrets, or sensitive personal information in public issues.

## Make a file change

1. Read the relevant guide in the [documentation index](docs/README.md).
2. Keep a copy or Git revision of the starting point.
3. Make a focused change and explain the need it addresses.
4. Check the result in context. For a page, read it on the running website; for an
   interaction, try the keyboard and narrow-screen layout too.
5. Review the changed files for unintended edits and private information.
6. Share the change through a GitHub pull request, which is a proposed set of edits
   for others to review. Explain what changed, why, and what you checked.

If you use Git locally, `git status` lists changed files and `git diff` shows
changes to tracked files. New files need separate review. You can also describe an
issue without preparing a pull request.

## Care for language, access, and shared decisions

English editorial changes stay English-only until translation is explicitly
requested. Preserve existing translations when changing shared software. Interface
and functionality improvements apply across all language editions.

Use clear headings, descriptive links, labelled controls, and instructions that do
not assume sight, a mouse, a fast connection, or specialist knowledge. Explain
technical terms when someone needs them. Use the Oxford comma. Keep the South African
adult-education purpose central, and describe international experience as a resource
for inquiry rather than a model everyone must adopt.

For software changes, follow [the development requirements](AGENTS.md). The target is
WCAG 2.2 Level AA; automated checks support, but do not establish, accessibility.
Record what you tested, what remains untested, and any known barriers.

## Respect the terms of shared work

Read the [licence guide](LICENSING.md) before contributing or reusing material.
First-party editorial content and software have different licences; third-party
material retains its own terms. Identify the source and permission for anything
you introduce. Do not submit material you are not entitled to share.
