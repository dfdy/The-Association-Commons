# Suggest a language

Suggest a language you would like to help make available. Suggestions help identify interest; they do not automatically create a translated edition. To offer practical help with translation, use the [Contact page](/en/contact).

Accepted names come from the bundled IANA Language Subtag Registry snapshot, based on ISO language codes. A registered language is not necessarily an official language in a particular country.

## Space for languages and communities pushed to the margins

South Africa's linguistic life extends beyond its official languages and standardised forms. We acknowledge the marginalisation of languages, dialects, and other language varieties through colonialism, apartheid, and continuing inequalities in education, public life, publishing, and access to resources. Different communities have experienced these exclusions in different ways; a lack of institutional recognition does not diminish their language or their right to participate.

We particularly welcome participation from speakers and communities connected with:

- **Khoe and San languages and traditions:** Nama (Khoekhoegowab), Nǀuu, ǃXun, Khwedam (Khwe), Xri, Tum'i, and Kora (ǃOra / Korana).
- **Nguni languages and varieties:** isiBhaca, isiHlubi, isiMpondo, isiMpondomise, and siPhuthi.
- **Sotho-Tswana languages and varieties:** Khelobedu (Selobedu), Sepulana (Sepulane), Setlokwa / SeṰokwa, and Sehananwa.
- **Other South African languages and varieties:** Kaaps, Cape Muslim Afrikaans, Iscamtho, Tsotsitaal, Flaaitaal, Griqua Afrikaans, Namaqualand Afrikaans, and Orange River Afrikaans. We also welcome work with the Arabic-Afrikaans writing tradition and its literary heritage.

These names are an invitation, not a definitive classification or an exhaustive list. Communities should help determine how their languages are named, written, represented, and developed. Participation should not depend on proving that a language is sufficiently different from a recognised standard, securing official status, or appearing in a registry.

**Propose and take collective ownership of a translation project.** We invite speakers, writers, educators, language activists, and community associations to lead translations, review existing drafts, develop shared terminology, and decide together how their language edition should work. This includes improving the existing Kaaps edition. Translation projects should be shaped and governed by their participants, with room for different usages and ways of contributing.

Use the [Contact page](/en/contact) to express interest. Tell us which language or variety you would like to work with and how you or your community would like to participate. You can propose a project even if the suggestion form above cannot match its name. No completed translation or institutional affiliation is needed to start a conversation; submitting the form does not automatically create a project or confer membership.
